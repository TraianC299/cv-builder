If you want your application to rank better in ATS systems and match what recruiters search for, your wording matters as much as your experience.

In 2026, users still search for terms like **resume builder**, **resume builder free**, **ATS resume template**, and **how to write a resume with no work experience**. Those phrases reveal what employers expect to see in modern CVs.

## High-Intent English CV Search Terms

Based on current autocomplete trends, these terms have strong intent:

- resume builder
- resume builder free
- ATS resume template
- ATS resume checker
- how to write a resume for a job
- how to write a resume summary
- how to write a resume with no work experience

Use these phrases as a signal for section naming and wording, not as spam.

## Where to Place Keywords in Your CV

Add relevant terms in these sections:

- Job title line: mirror the role name from the job ad
- Professional summary: include 2-3 role-specific terms
- Skills section: combine tools, methods, and domain language
- Experience bullets: use action + context + measurable result

Example:

- Weak: Responsible for support tasks
- Strong: Resolved 40+ support tickets daily in Zendesk, maintaining 98% CSAT

## ATS Keyword Checklist (Quick Version)

Before sending your CV, check:

- Is the target job title present in the summary?
- Are required tools and hard skills included exactly as written?
- Did you avoid generic phrases like "various duties"?
- Are achievements quantified with %, $, time, or volume?
- Is your file clean, readable, and ATS-safe (PDF with simple layout)?

## Final Tip

Keyword strategy is not about repeating the same phrase. It is about speaking the same language as the recruiter and ATS.

Use relevant search-driven wording, tailor each application, and keep your CV specific to the role.
