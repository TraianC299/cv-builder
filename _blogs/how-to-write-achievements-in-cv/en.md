One of the biggest mistakes job seekers make is listing only their **duties** in a CV — not their **achievements**.

Recruiters don’t just want to know what you were responsible for — they want to see the **impact** you made.

Here’s how to write effective achievements in your CV, with real-world examples.

## 🧠 Responsibilities vs. Achievements

- **Responsibility**: “Managed social media accounts.”
- **Achievement**: “Grew Instagram following by 65% in 6 months through targeted content.”

Achievements are measurable results, not job descriptions.

## 📌 Tips for Writing Powerful Achievements

1. **Use Action Verbs**  
   Start with words like “Increased,” “Led,” “Launched,” “Improved,” “Reduced,” “Designed,” etc.

2. **Quantify Results**  
   Add numbers, percentages, timeframes, or comparisons:

   > “Increased sales by 20% over 3 months” sounds stronger than “Helped boost sales.”

3. **Focus on Outcomes**  
   What happened because of your actions?

   > Instead of: “Trained new staff” → try: “Trained 5 new staff members, reducing onboarding time by 40%.”

4. **Tailor to the Job**  
   Prioritize achievements that align with what the company is looking for.

5. **Don’t Overdo It**  
   2–4 bullet points per job is enough. Keep them concise and relevant.

## ✅ Real CV Achievement Examples

- “Reduced customer response time by 50% by implementing a new ticketing system.”
- “Led redesign of company website, increasing average session duration by 30%.”
- “Closed €250,000 in new B2B contracts within first 6 months.”
- “Translated 15 technical documents from German to English with 100% accuracy.”

## 🧩 Final Tip

When in doubt, use this formula:  
**[Action Verb] + [What you did] + [Result/Impact]**

Turn boring bullet points into value-driven statements, and you’ll stand out instantly.

Your CV should show not only where you’ve worked — but what you’ve **accomplished**.
