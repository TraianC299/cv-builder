The skills section of your CV might seem simple — but it’s one of the first things recruiters scan. So which matters more: soft skills or hard skills?

Let’s break it down.

## What Are Hard Skills?

Hard skills are specific, teachable abilities. They're easy to quantify, test, or prove. Examples include:

- Programming languages (e.g., JavaScript, Python)
- Graphic design (e.g., Adobe Illustrator)
- Data analysis
- Accounting
- Foreign languages

You usually learn hard skills through courses, training, or job experience.

## What Are Soft Skills?

Soft skills are personal traits that affect how you work and interact with others. They’re harder to measure, but equally important. Examples:

- Communication
- Teamwork
- Problem-solving
- Leadership
- Time management

You build soft skills through everyday experiences, not just formal education.

## Which Do Recruiters Want?

**Both.** Most roles require a combination of technical ability (hard skills) and people-oriented traits (soft skills). For example:

- A web developer needs to know JavaScript (hard skill) but also collaborate with a design team (soft skill).
- A project manager needs tools like Trello (hard) and conflict resolution (soft).

## How to Include Skills in Your CV

1. **Match the Job Description**  
   Look at the required skills and mirror that language in your CV.

2. **Be Specific, Not Generic**  
   Instead of “Good communication,” write “Led weekly team briefings for 12+ staff.”

3. **Use a Skills Section + Evidence in Experience**  
   List your core skills, but also show them in action in your job descriptions.

4. **Balance Both Types**  
   Aim for a healthy mix — don't overload your CV with only one type.

## Final Tip

Use the skills section strategically. It’s not about listing everything you know — it’s about showing what matters **for the role you want**.

Skills tell your story just as much as experience does. Make them count.
