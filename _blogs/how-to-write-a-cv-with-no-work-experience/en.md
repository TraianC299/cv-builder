If you're a student or recent graduate, writing a CV with little or no work experience might feel daunting. But don't worry — everyone starts somewhere! Here’s how to create a strong, professional CV even if you're just getting started:

1. **Start with a Personal Statement**  
   Begin your CV with a short paragraph summarizing who you are, your strengths, and what kind of opportunity you’re seeking.

2. **Emphasize Education and Training**  
   If you don’t have work experience, your academic background is key. Mention relevant coursework, projects, certifications, and academic achievements.

3. **Highlight Transferable Skills**  
   Think about what you’ve gained from school, volunteering, part-time jobs, or extracurriculars: teamwork, communication, organization, problem-solving, etc.

4. **Include Volunteer Work and Internships**  
   Even unpaid experiences show initiative and help demonstrate your value. Treat them like any job — with role descriptions and results.

5. **Add a Projects or Portfolio Section**  
   Include personal, school, or group projects that relate to the job field. This is especially valuable for design, tech, or creative roles.

6. **Use a Clean and Modern Layout**  
   A well-formatted CV builds credibility. Keep it 1 page, easy to scan, and consistent in structure.

7. **Tailor the CV to the Job**  
   Analyze the job description and include keywords that match your skills or experience, even if it’s from a different context.

8. **Include a Skills Section**  
   Add both soft and hard skills: time management, adaptability, computer skills, languages, tools, etc.

9. **Write a Strong Ending**  
   End with a confident line such as “Open to entry-level roles in [industry]” or “Available immediately for internships or part-time roles.”

10. **Proofread Before Sending**  
   Check for typos, grammar issues, and formatting errors. A mistake-free CV shows professionalism and attention to detail.

No experience? No problem. The key is to shift the focus from what you haven’t done to what you **can** do. With the right structure and content, your first CV can still open doors.
